The question remains

An opening note on why some technical questions deserve to survive the closure of the story that first brought them into view.

20 September 20264 min readSide quests
Software engineeringExplorationField notes

OF
CONTEXT_Exploration

A story needs a boundary. Without one, a small fix can turn into an expedition across three system layers, two specifications and a decision that looked obvious until it did not.

That boundary is useful. So is curiosity.

ORIGINAL SCOPE

Finish the story with a contained, verifiable change.

OUT OF SCOPE

Understand the interesting behaviour around the problem, even when that understanding is no longer required to deliver the story.

Where this comes from

Questions appear while building software and not all of them are blockers. Sometimes a sufficient explanation fits in one line. Sometimes it requires isolating the behaviour, building a minimal test and comparing alternatives without the pressure of an immediate delivery.

The story closes. The question stays in the notebook.

OBSERVATION

A deferred question is not a discarded question. It only needs a different context and pace.

What I want to preserve

Out of Context is that second context: the blog for technical explorations, experiments and notes that start with concrete problems but do not try to turn every finding into a universal rule.

The structure will follow the investigation, not the other way around. Some entries will contain reproducible experiments. Others will be postmortems or short notes. Every article will have a hand-written summary and keep its remaining questions visible.

QUESTION

What can we learn when we stop optimising the exploration to fit inside the original story?

How it will read

The content lives in a single MDX source. AntoñiOS is where it is discovered; the full publication is where it is read with space, hierarchy and context. There are no two articles or two indexable URLs competing with each other.

LOG, EXPERIMENT, OBSERVATION and OPEN QUESTIONS are reading signals. They are not a rigid template.
